How to play Daily Word

Daily Word gives you six attempts to find a secret five-letter word. Type any valid five-letter English word and press Enter. The tiles then flip over one by one and change color to tell you exactly how close you are. A mint tile means that letter is correct and sitting in exactly the right position. An amber tile means the letter appears somewhere in the answer, but not in the spot where you placed it. A dark slate tile means the letter does not appear in the answer at all — or, if you used the same letter twice, that the answer contains fewer copies than you guessed.

Duplicate letters follow one simple rule: feedback never shows more copies of a letter than the answer actually contains. If the answer is SPARK and you guess PAPER, only one P earns a color because the answer holds just one P. The on-screen keyboard remembers everything you have learned, recoloring keys as information arrives, so you always have a live map of which letters remain in play.

Everyone in the world receives the same word on the same date, and a fresh one unlocks at midnight local time. You get one attempt per day — that scarcity is what makes a solve feel earned. If you want an extra challenge, switch on Hard Mode in settings: every revealed hint must then be reused in all later guesses, which forbids lazy letter-elimination turns. If you are stuck, a single optional hint reveals the first letter, and the game honestly records that you used it.

Tips & strategy

  • Open with vowel-rich, consonant-diverse words. Starters such as RAISE, ADIEU, SLATE or CRANE test five of the most frequent letters in one move.
  • Use your second guess to scan new letters. Unless hard mode is on, a word like MOUNT or GLINT after RAISE covers ten unique letters in two turns.
  • Think in positions, not just letters. An amber R tells you more than “R exists” — it eliminates R from that exact column. Track where letters cannot go.
  • Beware of repeats. Words like LEVEL, SASSY and MAMMA are rare but legal answers. If four tiles lock in and nothing fits, try doubling a confirmed letter.
  • Common endings win games. -ER, -LY, -ED and -ING patterns (minus the G) resolve many endgames; so do consonant blends like ST, CH, BR and PL at the start.

Daily Word FAQ

Can the answer contain the same letter twice?
Yes. Answers may contain repeated letters. The coloring never shows more copies of a letter than the answer actually contains, so one mint P and one slate P means exactly one P.
What counts as a valid guess?
Any five-letter word in our dictionary of several thousand common English words. If a word is rejected, it simply is not in the list — try a more common spelling.
What does Hard Mode change?
Hard Mode requires every revealed hint to be used in subsequent guesses: mint letters must stay in place and amber letters must appear somewhere. It is the purist’s way to play.
Does using a hint affect my stats?
Using the first-letter hint still lets you win, but the hint is counted in your statistics, so you will always know which solves were fully unaided.
Is the word the same for everyone?
Yes. The word is chosen deterministically from the date, so players everywhere face the same challenge and can fairly compare results.

How to play Daily Number

Daily Number hands you five numbers and one target. Your job is to combine the numbers with addition, subtraction, multiplication, division and parentheses to land exactly on the target — or as close as you possibly can. Tap a number chip to add it to your expression, tap an operator to join numbers together, and watch the live preview update with every move. Each number may be used at most once, but you are never required to use all five; a tidy three-number solution is just as valid as an elaborate five-number one.

The puzzle is generated from the date itself, and the generator always builds the target out of the given numbers first — which means a perfect, exact solution is guaranteed to exist for every single daily puzzle. If you finish with an exact hit you score a full 100 points. Land nearby and your score falls gently with the distance, so a near-miss of one or two still earns respectable points. After you submit, the game reveals one valid optimal path to the target so you can compare approaches and pick up new tricks for tomorrow.

There is no timer and no guess limit before you submit — Daily Number rewards patient, playful arithmetic rather than speed. Use Undo to step back one token or Clear to start fresh as often as you like. The only thing that counts is the expression you finally submit, once per day.

Tips & strategy

  • Anchor on the big number. Multiply or divide with the largest value first to get into the target’s neighborhood, then fine-tune with small numbers.
  • Think in remainders. Compute target − (your big product) and ask whether the leftover numbers can build that difference.
  • Division is a scalpel. Exact divisions like 75 ÷ 5 or 100 ÷ 4 unlock values the original set doesn’t contain.
  • Parentheses change everything. (7 + 3) × 9 and 7 + 3 × 9 are 90 and 34 — group additions before multiplying to reach round numbers fast.
  • Close is still points. If the exact target refuses to appear, submit your best near-miss instead of nothing; a distance of 3 still scores well.

Daily Number FAQ

Do I have to use all five numbers?
No. Use any subset — each number at most once. Many targets fall to a clever three-number combination.
Is every puzzle actually solvable?
Yes. The generator constructs the target by applying random valid operations to the day’s numbers, so an exact solution always exists, and one is revealed after you finish.
Are fractions or negative numbers allowed mid-expression?
Yes — intermediate values can be anything. Only your final result is compared with the target.
How is the score calculated?
An exact hit scores 100. Otherwise the score decreases with your distance from the target, down to a minimum of zero.
Can I retry after submitting?
No — one submission per day, which is what makes an exact hit worth celebrating. You can rebuild and preview as much as you like before submitting.

How to play Daily Trivia

Daily Trivia serves five multiple-choice questions drawn from a large original bank spanning science, geography, history, pop culture and the natural world. The five questions are selected deterministically from the date, so every player answers the same quiz on the same day — your score is directly comparable with anyone else’s.

Each question gives you four options and fifteen seconds, counted down by a slim ring around the timer. Tap an answer to lock it in: the correct option instantly glows mint, a wrong pick glows coral, and a short “Did you know?” fact appears so you leave every question — right or wrong — knowing a little more than before. If the ring empties before you choose, the question is marked incorrect and the quiz moves on, so stay decisive. There is no penalty for guessing; an educated guess beats a timeout every time.

After the fifth question you receive a score out of five and a rank title: Rookie for 0–1, Thinker for 2–3, Sharp Mind for 4, and the coveted Genius for a flawless 5. Finishing trivia counts toward your daily streak, and completing it alongside the word and number puzzles earns the Daily Trio badge on your stats page. Like the other games, trivia can be played once per day and resets at midnight local time.

Tips & strategy

  • Read the question twice, fast. Most fifteen-second mistakes are misread questions, not missing knowledge — watch for “NOT”, “largest” and “first”.
  • Eliminate before you choose. Striking out two obviously wrong options turns a 25% blind guess into a 50% coin flip.
  • Trust your first instinct. Recognition memory is quick and usually right; last-second switches are wrong more often than not.
  • Never let the clock expire. A timeout scores zero with certainty; any tap gives you at least a chance.
  • Read the facts. The “Did you know?” lines are tomorrow’s answers in disguise — the question bank loves themes.

Daily Trivia FAQ

Are the questions the same for everyone?
Yes. The day’s date selects five questions from the bank, so everyone worldwide plays the identical quiz that day.
What happens when the timer runs out?
The question is marked as unanswered and counts as incorrect. The correct answer is revealed before the next question begins.
Can I change my answer?
No — the first option you tap locks in immediately and feedback is shown at once, which keeps the quiz brisk and honest.
What are the rank titles?
Rookie (0–1 correct), Thinker (2–3), Sharp Mind (4) and Genius (a perfect 5).
Where do the questions come from?
All questions are written originally for DailyBrain and fact-checked for accuracy. They are family-friendly and span five rotating categories.

About DailyBrain

DailyBrain began with a simple observation: the best part of many people’s morning is a tiny ritual — one coffee, one puzzle, one small win before the day begins. We wanted to build that ritual properly: beautiful, fast, respectful of your time and your privacy, and genuinely fun to return to.

Every midnight, three new challenges appear: a five-letter word hunt, a numbers round inspired by classic arithmetic game shows, and a five-question trivia quiz written originally for this site. Each is deterministic — generated from the date itself — so a player in London, a player in Toronto and a player in Sydney all face exactly the same puzzle on the same calendar day.

Your companion through all of it is Spark, the little neuron that lives in our logo. Spark evolves with your streak — from a sleepy grey ember to a twinkling, ringed, orbiting supernova at thirty days. We built Spark because streaks should feel like nurturing something, not feeding a counter.

DailyBrain stores your progress in your own browser. We have no accounts, no profiles and no interest in your personal data. The site is supported by a small number of clearly-labelled advertisements that never interrupt play. If you have ideas, spot an error in a trivia question, or just want to share a 30-day Spark, we would love to hear from you on the contact page.
